👑 3. Dam and Secret Courtyard

Dam Square grew up by the dam on the Amstel that gave the city its name. The palace was built as the town hall, a symbol of Golden Age wealth. A couple of minutes from busy Kalverstraat hides the Begijnhof, a quiet courtyard where single religious women have lived since the Middle Ages.

Munttoren and flower market → Rokin → Spui → Begijnhof (enter from Gedempte Begijnensloot) → Vleminckx → back of the palace → Dam Square → LEGO Store, Kalverstraat 57. ~2 km, 2 h · Begijnhof daily 9:30–17:00; Vleminckx Sun from 12; LEGO Store Sun 11:30–18:30, so start at Munttoren ~11:00 · Fries ~5 € (two sauces a bit more); Pick & Build: price on the wall · Vleminckx · Cafés on Spui

  • 3.1 Zone Photo Hunt: 📍Munttoren, Muntplein — ✔ 1) Munttoren, Muntplein: the start. 2) Bloemenmarkt, Singel: stalls on barges, visible from the bridge at Muntplein. 3) Equestrian statue of Queen Wilhelmina, Rokin (between Muntplein and Spui). 4) Nationaal Monument, Dam Square: the 22 m white pillar. 5) Magna Plaza, Nieuwezijds Voorburgwal 182, opposite the back of the palace (3.5). 6) Nieuwe Kerk, by the palace on Dam. No timer: done if all photos are in before the boss.
    1) Munttoren is what's left of the medieval Regulierspoort gate (1480s); after a fire in 1618 Hendrick de Keyser added the tower on top. In 1672–73, when war cut off the way to the mint, coins were struck here, hence the "Mint Tower". For the kid: "They made real coins here, like yours!" 2) Bloemenmarkt, since 1862: flowers came by boat, and the stalls still stand on barges. It is the only floating flower market in the world. 3) Wilhelmina reigned for 50 years (1898–1948), longer than anyone in the country; during the war she spoke to the Dutch by radio from London. The statue is from 1972. 4) Nationaal Monument (1956) honours those who died in the Second World War; the wall holds urns with soil from every province. On 4 May at 20:00 the whole country keeps two minutes' silence here. 5) Magna Plaza (1899) was built as the main post office, which is why it looks like a palace with turrets; now it's a shopping centre, and you can look at the dome inside. 6) Nieuwe Kerk ("New" since the 1400s!): kings are crowned and royals married here; today it is an exhibition hall.
  • 3.2 Bronze Rascal: 📍Het Lieverdje, Spui — ✔ "Het Lieverdje" (1960). A parent watches the frame and adjusts until it matches. A lorry knocked the statue over in March 2024; it was repaired and returned to Spui in May 2024.
    "Lieverdje" ("little darling") was what journalist Henri Knap called Amsterdam's street kids in 1947: naughty but good-hearted. The statue is by Carel Kneulman; readers' donations didn't cover the bronze, so the cigarette firm Crescent paid. That's why in the 1960s the "anti-smoking magician" Robert Jasper Grootveld held night-time happenings at the statue against smoking and consumerism; clashes with police around them gave rise to the Provo movement. The statue has been painted, dressed up, once kidnapped, and knocked over by lorries in 2012 and 2024. It is part of the Amsterdam Canon. For the kid: "This rascal stands for all the naughty kids of Amsterdam. Like you!"
  • 3.3 Secret Courtyard: 📍Begijnhof, Het Houten Huys — ✔ Begijnhof, house no. 34, about 500 years old. People live in the courtyard, so speak quietly! Enter through the gate on Gedempte Begijnensloot (the Spui door is closed to visitors). Daily 9:30–17:00 (summer until 18), free, no bikes or large groups.
    The courtyard is first mentioned in 1389; it was home to Beguines, Catholic laywomen who lived alone, prayed and nursed the sick but were free to leave and marry. The last Beguine died in 1971; single women live here now. No. 34 is one of two wooden houses in the centre (the other is In 't Aepjen, zone 4); only its front and back walls are wooden, and the upper floor juts out in medieval fashion. The date is disputed: around 1420 or around 1528. Wooden houses were later banned because of fires. Behind the ordinary facades of nos. 29–30 hides a Catholic "hidden church" (1671–80). The Spui door was only added in the 19th century; visitors now enter from Gedempte Begijnensloot. For the kid: "This wood is 500 years old, and the house is still standing!"
  • 3.4 Blindfold Sauce: 📍Vleminckx, Voetboogstraat 33 — ✔ Any two sauces. Oorlog ("war"): mayonnaise, peanut satay sauce and raw onion. Vleminckx: Sat 11–19, Sun 12–19 (unofficial), usually a queue.
    Vleminckx is a tiny window selling Flemish fries, since 1957 according to most sources. Nicknamed the "Sausmeester" ("sauce master") for its dozens of sauces. A popular choice is "oorlog" ("war"): mayonnaise, peanut sauce and onion. For the kid: "Fries with a sauce called 'war'!"
  • 3.5 Animals from Faraway Lands: 📍Koninklijk Paleis, rear facade (Nieuwezijds Voorburgwal) — ✔ The rear pediment "The Four Continents Bring Gifts to Amsterdam": the animals include an elephant and a lion (Africa), a camel and a crocodile with a boy riding it (Asia), plus small ones like a parrot. We found no exact number: it counts when the kid's and the parent's totals match. Watch from the pavement: trams run on Nieuwezijds Voorburgwal.
    The palace was built in 1648–1665 as the town hall, then the largest city government building in Europe; it became a palace under Louis Bonaparte in 1808. The bronze statues on the roof, designed by Artus Quellinus, were cast by the Hemony brothers. Atlas (about 6 m; the globe almost 4 m and about 1000 kg) stands at the back between Temperance and Vigilance. In July 2024 the globe was taken down for restoration; in spring 2026 Atlas returned, dark again, while the globe stayed light green. The weather vane is a cog ship with two little men and a dog: legend says a rudderless boat ran aground where Amsterdam was founded. For the kid: "The city was so rich it carried the whole world on its shoulders!"
