🖼️ 10. Rijksmuseum and De Pijp

De Pijp ("the Pipe") grew up in the late 19th century on fields with windmills as a working-class district, partly for the Heineken brewery; one story says its long narrow streets "blew" people out to work like a pipe blows smoke. Metro 52 was dug right under the old city.

Metro 52 → De Pijp → Sarphatipark → walk ~15 min to Museumplein (slide, lyre) → tunnel → Rijksmuseum (Night Watch, doll's house). 3 h. The zone can't be won without the museum: book a slot · Rijksmuseum daily 9–17, time slot for everyone; the rest is outdoors · Under 18 free, adult €25 (Museumkaart valid) · Cafés on Museumplein; Albert Cuyp market by the metro (Mon–Sat) as a bonus · Rijksmuseum

  • 10.1 Giant Escalator: 📍De Pijp metro station — ✔ Depth ~27 m, a canal house ~12–14 m → ≈ 2 houses. For fun, also time the escalator ride up.
    De Pijp is the deepest station in the Netherlands (~27 m): metro 52 runs under old houses and canals. While the nearby Vijzelgracht station was being built, a leak in the construction pit lowered the groundwater and old houses sank twice; work was halted, residents were evacuated, and the wall joints were later frozen with machines. For the kid: "There are canals and old houses above us!"
  • 10.2 Zone Photo Hunt: 📍Albert Cuypmarkt — ✔ In route order: 1) Albert Cuypmarkt: 150 m north of De Pijp metro (market Mon–Sat 9–17; no stalls on Sunday, then photograph the street sign). 2) Heineken Experience, Stadhouderskade 78: a ~250 m detour from Ruysdaelkade. 3) Moco Museum, Villa Alsberg, Honthorststraat 20, by Museumplein. 4) Stedelijk Museum, Museumplein 10: the white "bathtub" (de Badkuip). 5) Van Gogh Museum, Museumplein 6: the new wing is oval. No time limit: it counts if all the photos are taken before the boss.
    1) Albert Cuypmarkt is the largest street market in the Netherlands (since 1905), 260 stalls: cheese, herring, stroopwafels fresh off the griddle. It is named after a 17th-century painter, like all the streets in De Pijp. For the kid: "All the streets here are named after painters!" 2) Heineken Experience: the old Heineken brewery (1867–1988), now a museum; inside stand huge copper vats where the beer was brewed, and it was delivered around town on horse-drawn carts. 3) Moco Museum: in a 1904 villa with a tower that looks like a little castle; inside are Banksy and Dalí. 4) Stedelijk: the museum of modern art; Amsterdammers immediately nicknamed its new white wing (2012) "the bathtub". 5) Van Gogh Museum: the most Van Gogh paintings in the world (about 200); the oval wing (1999) was built by the Japanese architect Kisho Kurokawa. For the kid: "They say Van Gogh sold only one painting in his whole life!"
  • 10.3 Park Temple: 📍Sarphatimonument, Sarphatipark — ✔ Shields: "Volksvlyt – Onderwijs – Handel" (people's industry, education, trade; "y" is an old spelling of "ij"). On the cornice: "Sarphati – Stichter – Van 't Nieuw Amsterdam". Below: lion heads and frogs (the fountain may be off in winter).
    The monument to the doctor Samuel Sarphati (1813–1866) was put up in 1886: a temple ~12 m tall in the middle of a pool 16 m wide, columns of red Swedish granite, a bronze bust by Stracké-van Bosse. The cornice reads "Sarphati, founder of the new Amsterdam"; the shields on the dome say "Industry of the people, Education, Trade". Sarphati treated the poor and fought filth: he pushed for rubbish collection, opened a bread factory selling cheap bread, started the Palace of Industry and the Amstel Hotel, and came up with this park, but did not live to see it open. During the occupation the bust of the Jewish doctor was removed; it was put back after the liberation. For the kid: "This doctor looked after a whole city, he even got them to collect the rubbish!"
  • 10.4 ⏱ Slide and Tunnel: 📍Museumplein playground → Rijksmuseum passage — ✔ The playground was renewed in summer 2025: two slides, a net drum tower, a basket swing. The passage is free; stay on the pedestrian side (bikes!). From ~14.11 there is an ice rink on Museumplein: check the playground is open.
    From the start, Cuypers made the museum a gateway between the centre and the new south. The museum opened in 1885. During the renovation the Spanish architects wanted the main entrance in the passage, but residents and the Cyclists' Union saved the bike route; the entrance was put underneath it. The renewed museum reopened in 2013 after 10 years of work. For the kid: "The cyclists beat the museum!"
  • 10.5 Lyre on a Finger: 📍Museumplein, by the Rijksmuseum (view of the Concertgebouw) — ✔ A gilded lyre has topped the gable since 1888 (today's is a 1993 copy, ~3 m). Walk back towards the Rijksmuseum; the square has no cars, but bikes cross it on a path.
    The Concertgebouw (1888) was built on a marsh outside the city on 2186 piles; its Main Hall is considered one of the best-sounding in the world. Museumplein was waste ground for the 1883 World Exhibition. For the kid: "Music plays in this building, and the Rijksmuseum at the other end of the square is the house of paintings."
  • 10.6 Night Watch: 📍Rijksmuseum, Night Watch room — ✔ The girl in the golden dress is left of centre, the chicken on her belt; the dog at the bottom right barks at the drummer. The name came later, when the varnish darkened. The painting is being restored in a glass room (Operatie Nachtwacht, probably until 2027), visible through the glass. The Night Watch room is at the end of the Gallery of Honour (number on the map).
    "The Night Watch" (1642) is a group portrait of the militia company of Captain Frans Banninck Cocq. It is not a night scene: it got that name later, when the varnish darkened. The girl's chicken is the sign of the arquebusiers' company: its claws were their emblem. Rembrandt took the dog from a drawing by Adriaen van de Venne. Since November 2024 restorers have been removing the old varnish right in the room, in a glass chamber, and the painting stays on the wall. For the kid: "Find who is the noisiest here: the drummer or the dog!"
  • 👑 Doll's House: 📍Rijksmuseum, Petronella Oortman's doll's house — ✔ Petronella Oortman's doll's house, room 2.20: 9 rooms, a cabinet with tortoiseshell and pewter inlay. Make up your own questions by looking at the house while he looks (kitchen, library, nurse with baby, baskets, paintings).
    It is not a toy but a rich lady's collector's cabinet: Petronella Oortman put it together around 1686–1710. The oak cabinet is inlaid with tortoiseshell and pewter; inside are silk, velvet, glass, marble, copper, all in exact proportion. According to her daughter it cost about 30,000 guilders, as much as a canal house. The original dolls are lost. For the kid: "They paid as much for this doll's house as for a real one!"
