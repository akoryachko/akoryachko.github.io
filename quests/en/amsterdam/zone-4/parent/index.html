🏯 4. Nieuwmarkt and the Waag

Zeedijk is a former sea dyke, the oldest part of the city by the port: sailors left for sea from here and came back here. The Waag and the Schreierstoren are pieces of the medieval city wall. Today Zeedijk is the Chinatown.

A loop: He Hua Temple (Zeedijk) → along Zeedijk, Nieuwebrugsteeg and the edge of Warmoesstraat to Tony's Chocolonely (Oudebrugsteeg) → along Damrak and Prins Hendrikkade to the Schreierstoren → Scheepvaarthuis → along Oudeschans past Montelbaanstoren, Zuiderkerk and the VOC gate → Kloveniersburgwal 26 → Waag. ~3.2 km, 2.5 h. Don't turn south of Oudebrugsteeg on Warmoesstraat or west of Zeedijk: that is the red-light district · Tony's Chocolonely Super Store: daily from 10:00, closing 17:00–21:00 depending on the day (check tonyschocolonely.com). Everything else is outdoors · 5 Tony's bars ≈ €20–25; everything else free · Chocolate from Tony's; Chinese bakeries and cafés on Zeedijk (optional) · Cafés on Nieuwmarkt

  • 4.1 Zone photo hunt: 📍He Hua Temple, Zeedijk 106–118 — ✔ In route order: 1) He Hua Temple, Zeedijk 106–118, the start of the zone; bonus shot: a roof corner "touching" the Waag. 2) In 't Aepjen, Zeedijk 1, at the end of Zeedijk, on the way to Tony's. 3) Basiliek van de Heilige Nicolaas, Prins Hendrikkade 73, on the way from Tony's to the Schreierstoren. 4) Montelbaanstoren, Oudeschans 2, from the Scheepvaarthuis (4.4) along Oudeschans. 5) Zuiderkerk, Zuiderkerkhof, on the way. 6) Oost-Indisch Huis: the VOC gate at Oude Hoogstraat 24, by the bridge to Kloveniersburgwal. No time limit: it counts if all photos are taken before the boss.
    1) He Hua Temple: a Buddhist temple in the style of a Chinese palace, opened in 2000 by Queen Beatrix; said to be the largest of its kind in Europe. You may go in quietly, no photos of people. For the kid: "This is how palaces are built in China: the roof corners point at the sky!" 2) In 't Aepjen (1546–1550): one of the last two wooden houses in the centre (the other is in the Begijnhof, zone 3); legend says sailors paid for their bed here with monkeys, hence "At the Little Monkeys". 3) Basilica of St Nicholas (1887): the main Catholic church of the centre. Nicholas is the patron saint of Amsterdam and of sailors, and he is Sinterklaas too. For the kid: "This is Sinterklaas's church!" 4) Montelbaanstoren (1516) guarded the shipyards; in 1606 Hendrick de Keyser added its openwork top, and in 1611 the tower began to lean and was pulled straight with ropes. 5) Zuiderkerk (1611): the city's first Protestant church, its tower also by Hendrick de Keyser; Claude Monet painted it from the Groenburgwal canal, and Rembrandt lived nearby. 6) Oost-Indisch Huis (1606): headquarters of the Dutch East India Company (VOC), the world's first company with shares; its ships sailed to Indonesia for spices. Today it is part of the university.
  • 4.2 Blind chocolate tasting: 📍Tony's Chocolonely Super Store, Oudebrugsteeg 15 — ✔ The kid picks 5 different flavours himself (a 180 g bar ≈ €4–5). The parent remembers the order (or notes it on the phone). Taste on a bench by the water at the Schreierstoren, the next stop (5 minutes along Prins Hendrikkade). 4 out of 5 counts; fewer, and you can try again later with other pieces.
    Tony's Chocolonely was founded in 2005 by Dutch journalist Teun van de Keuken: he learned that cocoa beans are often picked by child slaves in West Africa and decided to make slave-free chocolate. The bar is divided into unequal pieces on purpose, just as the money from cocoa is shared unequally. For the kid: "Look, all the pieces are different. Can you guess why?"
  • 4.3 Traveller's Tower: 📍Schreierstoren — ✔ The stone of 1569 (a weeping woman and a ship on the waves). On the left of the tower is a 1927 plaque to Hudson; there is also a stone for the first voyage to the East Indies (1595).
    This defence tower of about 1487 stood where the wall made a sharp turn, hence its old name Schrayershoucktoren, "sharp corner tower". Wikipedia calls the lovely legend of sailors' wives "weeping" (schreien) here a myth, but the 1569 stone with a weeping woman is there. It is Amsterdam's only surviving defence tower; until 1960 it held a harbour office, now a café. On 4 April 1609 Henry Hudson left from here on the "Halve Maen" to look for a route to Asia, and found the river where New Amsterdam, the future New York, later grew. For the kid: "A ship left from here and found the place for New York!"
  • 4.4 Sea monsters: 📍Scheepvaarthuis, Prins Hendrikkade 108 — ✔ The facade is covered in stone and iron carvings: sea monsters, dolphins, Neptune, seahorses, ships and maps. The kid picks the scariest. Look from the pavement of Prins Hendrikkade (a busy street).
    The Scheepvaarthuis (1913–1916) was built by six shipping companies: the first building of the "Amsterdam School" (architects Van der Mey, De Klerk and Kramer). It stands on the site of the shipyards and warehouses from which the first ships left for the East Indies in 1595. Today it is a hotel. For the kid: "The whole house tells about the sea, like a book made of stone."
  • 4.5 Door-Wide House: 📍Kleine Trippenhuis, Kloveniersburgwal 26 — ✔ Kleine Trippenhuis 2.44 m (≈ 2 of the kid's arm spans); the Trippenhuis (no. 29) opposite is over 22 m with 7 windows in a row → ≈ 9 little houses. The roof chimneys are shaped like mortars. The coachman legend was made up later.
    The Trippenhuis was built in 1660–62 for the brothers Louys and Hendrick Trip, traders in weapons, copper and tar: two mirror-image houses behind one facade, with chimneys shaped like mortars. Over 22 m wide, it is the widest house in the city; from 1816 to 1885 it held the Rijksmuseum with the "Night Watch", today the Academy of Sciences. Opposite is the Kleine Trippenhuis, 2.44 m wide (widening to ~6 m at the back). The legend of the coachman sighing "I wish I had a house as wide as your door" is made up: the little house was finished in 1696, when both brothers were already dead. For the kid: "How many little houses fit into the big one? Let's measure!"
  • 👑 ⏱ Guild masters: 📍De Waag, Nieuwmarkt — ✔ Above the doors are 4 guilds: smiths, painters, masons, surgeons; Theatrum Anatomicum is the surgeons' entrance (skull and instruments). Pick details visible from the square, one each on different doors.
    This was the city gate Sint-Antoniespoort; it is traditionally dated 1488, but historians allow ~1425. It is the oldest non-church building in Amsterdam. In 1617–18 the gate became a weigh house, and upstairs four guilds moved in, each with its own entrance: smiths, painters, masons (their entrance decorated by Hendrick de Keyser) and surgeons. In the surgeons' hall bodies were dissected in anatomy lessons; the guild commissioned Rembrandt's "The Anatomy Lesson of Dr Nicolaes Tulp" (1632), which hung here until 1828. The domed anatomy theatre was added in the 1690s. Today there is a restaurant downstairs. For the kid: "Doctors learned here how a person works inside!"
