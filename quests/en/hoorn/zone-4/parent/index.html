🔑 Zone 4 — for parents

⚓ 4. Harbour

Hoorn's harbour was the heart of the Golden Age town: VOC ships and the whalers of the Northern Company sailed from here (from 1614 the company sat in the 1532 Hoofdtoren). The way there follows the eastern edge of the old town: past the Mariatoren (1508, part of the town wall, later a gunpowder store), through the park along the Draafsingel canal to the East Gate. Merchant houses line Veermanskade, and on the Hoofd sit the boys from the book about Captain Bontekoe, whose 1646 journal was a Dutch bestseller.

  • 4.1 Kibbeling Fish: 📍Hoogland Vis, Gedempte Turfhaven 36 — ✔ Usually cod (kabeljauw) or another white fish — see the sign. Hoogland Vis is open on Sundays 12–17 (chain's website; check they have kibbeling). Closed — buy it at the harbour if anything is open, or skip without a curse.
    Kibbeling is Dutch street food: bites of white fish in batter, deep-fried, with a garlic or remoulade sauce. The name used to mean the off-cuts of salted cod that fishermen and traders sold cheaply; today it's usually cod or saithe fillet. Fish kept harbour-town Hoorn alive for centuries: herring boats and whalers sailed from here. For the kid: "Kibbeling is the Dutch fishermen's popcorn!"
  • 4.2 ⏱ Powder for the Tower: 📍Oosterplantsoen by the Mariatoren — ✔ Leaves of different shapes (maple, oak, chestnut…); each player carries one at a time. Run only on the park paths, stay away from the canal edge; the parent keeps an eye on both. No bench — any "store" tree by the path.
    The round Mariatoren (1508) was part of the town wall; St Mary's convent paid for it, hence the name. Later it stored gunpowder (Kruittoren) and from 1900 ice. Today the Hendrick de Keyser society lets it as holiday rooms. The Oosterplantsoen park follows the Draafsingel canal, the old town moat. For the kid: "They kept gunpowder in this tower — that's why it has so few windows!"
  • 4.3 The Last Gate: 📍Oosterpoort, Bij Oosterpoort — ✔ The arches are visible side-on from the Vollerswaal path, not from the bridge itself; Wikipedia says 7 (bridge of 1763) — count together. The passage is curved (monument register) — one theory says so that a cannon couldn't fire straight into town. Bikes ride through the passage.
    The Oosterpoort ("East Gate") was built in 1578 to a design by J.J. Bilhamer; in 1601 a gatekeeper's house was added above the passage. It is Hoorn's only surviving city gate. It was less for defence than for checking visitors and collecting tolls. The passage is curved — one theory says so that a cannon could not fire straight into town. No cars since 1957; the Hendrick de Keyser society has owned it since 2003. For the kid: "A gate with a house on top — the gatekeeper lived right above the road!"
  • 4.4 Sea Battle: 📍Bossuhuizen, Slapershaven / Grote Oost 132 — ✔ The biggest ship is the admiral's (decide together from the carving). The corner house (Grote Oost 132) says 1612.
    The Bossuhuizen are named after the "Battle of Bossu": on 11 October 1573, on the Zuiderzee, small West Frisian ships beat the Spanish fleet and captured its admiral, Count Bossu, who was held in Hoorn for three years. The carved frieze above the ground floor tells the battle (monument register). The corner house, Grote Oost 132, is dated 1612 in the stones on its jutting upper floor. For the kid: "Little boats beat the big ships — and brought the admiral himself here as a prisoner!"
  • 4.5 The Drawbridge: 📍Hoogebrug bridge — ✔ The lifting part is ~7 m (monument register). 7 m ÷ the kid's height (help with the division): at 1.25 m, 5–6 times. Pace along the edge of the bridge, letting bikes and cars pass.
    The iron drawbridge Hoogebrug (1930) links Oude Doelenkade with Veermanskade: a 6 m opening, the lifting part ~7 m long and 4.5 m wide, a wooden deck with a 3.2 m roadway. Hoorn chose iron over the wooden bridge the heritage experts wanted — wood costs more to maintain. Built by Kloos of Kinderdijk. Beyond it lies Veermanskade with its merchant houses; one guidebook says Captain Bontekoe lived at no. 15. For the kid: "The bridge lifts so tall masts can pass!"
  • 👑 Fourth Ship's Boy: 📍Scheepsjongens van Bontekoe, Hoofd — ✔ The parent counts to 30, tries to make the kid laugh without touching, and guesses whom the kid copies. The quay has no railing: the kid stands on the house side, the parent between the kid and the edge. After the boss, show the statue of Captain Bontekoe himself (~80 m): where is he looking?
    The three bronze boys are Hajo, Rolf and Padde from Johan Fabricius's children's book "The Ship's Boys of Bontekoe" (1924). The book is based on the real journal of Captain Willem Bontekoe of Hoorn (1587–1657): his ship "Nieuw Hoorn" blew up on the way to Java, and the survivors starved and were saved on Sumatra; the 1646 journal was reprinted many times. The statue is by Jan van Druten and has stood here since 1968. Three footballers from Hoorn posed for it without knowing why, until Fabricius himself pulled off the cover: "That's us!" For the kid: "The boys the statue was modelled on only recognised themselves at the unveiling!" Next to it is the Hoofdtoren (1532): the Northern Company's whalers sat there from 1614, and it has been a restaurant since 1968. About 80 m away stands a statue of Captain Bontekoe himself — show it after the boss.

📜 How to play

  1. We are one team against Hoorn. 4 zones in one day, with a card for each.
  2. Challenge done? Tick the box ☐ and colour in the coins 🪙. You lead everyone to all the zone's stops with the map yourself? Another 🧭 +2.
  3. Got enough coins to reach the flag 🏁? The zone is CONQUERED! A sticker on the card and on the zone on the map.
  4. 👑 Boss: a big challenge. It may not work the first time, so try again! ⚔️ Duel: me against the parents. I get the coins for playing either way; the last one (+1) is only for winning. ⏱ Against the clock.
  5. The challenge number (1.2) is a stop on the map. Spend your coins or save them: the more you have left in the evening, the higher your rank!

🌀 City curses

A curse falls when we enter a new zone and the die shows 1, or when we skip a challenge. Roll the die again: the number is the curse. It hits the whole team until we finish the next challenge.

  1. We talk like pirates: "Arr!" at the end of every sentence
  2. We walk like seagulls, arms as wings
  3. See a boat or a train? Shout "Full steam ahead!"
  4. One hand behind your back
  5. The next challenge on one leg (where you can)
  6. We puff like a steam train: "Choo-choo!" at every corner
